Introduction

Criminals have thought up ways, for a variety of reasons, of gaining unauthorised access into just about anything for centuries. This overarching threat is ever-present in a modern world heavily reliant on wireless devices for means of finance, communication, data storage, entertainment and general productivity. However, despite the extensive good these devices have brought and continue to bring society, their greatest strength in being wireless is paradoxically their greatest weakness, with several recent attacks on internet connected devices (IoT devices) demonstrating this, and the potential consequences of these type of attacks resulting in damage to expensive equipment or even loss of life (Papp et al., 2019). The aim of this article is to emphasise the vulnerabilities in both every day and industrial machines and devices, presenting the means of discovering these vulnerabilities, as well as reflecting on two cases of real-world hardware security breaches in the Iranian nuclear centrifuge destroying Stuxnet worm and a very recent, at the time of writing, loophole discovered in modern contactless payments by the University of Surrey in collaboration with the University of Birmingham.
